abstract: "<p>Hong Kong's urban growth has depended on expanding its sources of fresh water beyond the territory's limited catchments. This chapter traces the shift from local reservoirs and colonial waterworks toward dependence on water imported from mainland China, particularly through the Dongjiang supply system. It shows how water scarcity, engineering decisions, and political relations with Guangdong have repeatedly reshaped Hong Kong's infrastructural geography. The history makes clear that water security is also a question of territorial dependence and political authority.</p>"
