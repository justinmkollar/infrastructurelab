abstract: "<p>This article critically examines the Sidewalk Labs smart city proposal in Toronto and its relationship to earlier traditions of technocratic urban management. It considers how claims about data-driven efficiency and innovation legitimized new arrangements between municipal government and technology corporations. Through the project's development and eventual abandonment, it evaluates conflicts over public authority, urban data, and democratic accountability in technology-led planning.</p>"
