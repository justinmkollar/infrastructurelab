abstract: "<p>Hong Kong's dense built environment brings public space into close contact with transport infrastructure, engineered waterfronts, and other urban systems. This chapter considers the informal negotiations through which people inhabit and adapt public spaces that are shaped by planning and infrastructure decisions. It draws attention to the everyday relationships between designed environments and patterns of public use. These relationships complicate assumptions that the public realm is determined solely by formal ownership or design.</p>"
