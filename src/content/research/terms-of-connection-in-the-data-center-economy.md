    body: <p>Data centers are becoming a major source of electricity demand,
      exposing gaps in the regulatory frameworks governing grid access,
      connection queues, infrastructure planning, and the allocation of
      electricity system costs. These pressures are producing divergent policy
      responses, from Ireland, where data centers accounted for 22% of national
      electricity demand in 2024 and new connections face requirements for
      additional generation and renewable procurement, to the United States,
      where federal regulators have directed regional grid operators to
      reconsider how large loads connect and pay for electricity infrastructure.
      This research examines how governments, regulators, utilities, and grid
      operators establish the conditions under which data centers can obtain
      power, including their obligations to finance network upgrades, procure
      generation, manage demand, and contribute to system reliability. Through a
      comparative, source-based database of electricity regulations, tariffs,
      and regulatory proceedings, it traces how these responsibilities are
      negotiated and distributed across different electricity markets and
      institutional settings. The project aims to make these complex regulatory
      arrangements accessible and comparable, identifying how different systems
      manage the growth of computing infrastructure and the consequences of
      these decisions for electricity consumers, public investment, and the
      wider energy transition.</p>
